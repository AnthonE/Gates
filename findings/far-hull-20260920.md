# The far tree hull, measured — 2026-09-20

**Every claim here carries the command that produced it** (`findings/README.md`).
Written off a playtest capture: the operator asked what was looking rough, the
box had never run the client, and nine frames of a populated shard plus six of
an empty island are what the answer came from. The decision is `DECISIONS.md`
§open, far hull v1.

## 0. How the frames were taken

This container had none of the seven things `CLAUDE.md` lists for a native
client. `apt-get install libwayland-dev libasound2-dev libudev-dev
libxkbcommon-x11-0 mesa-vulkan-drivers libvulkan1` plus `pip install Pillow
numpy` was the whole of it; Xvfb was already present.

```
./ci/scene.sh --kit "item.hatchet_stone:1"   # 6 inhabitants, a base
./ci/scene.sh --population 0 --settle 0      # empty island, clean vantages
```

⚠ **The populated run's frames are not a bar and must not be read as one.**
The probe spawned inside a bot's base and said so — `capture: still inside a
base after 20 frames — shooting the vantages from here, so they will show the
inside of a wall` — so a twig wall fills every vantage and drags p90, sky and
saturation for reasons that are not the renderer. The empty run is the one the
numbers below come from.

## 1. What the frame showed

Past the LOD swap every tree becomes a smooth faceted green capsule, and in a
forest frame the hulls outnumber the real trees several to one. They do not
read as a cheaper tree; they read as a different plant. The near trees are
good — ragged, layered, dark inside and lit on top, which is canopy grain v0
(2026-09-16) arriving.

## 2. The first instrument was wrong, in the way it warned about

`crates/client/examples/hull_profile.rs`, first draft: read the tree's
per-band radius at `IMPOSTOR_GIRTH_Q` and compare the hull to it.

| | per-band ratio | silhouette area |
|---|---|---|
| hull vs tree-at-quantile | 0.85–0.96 | **89–91 %** |

That says the hull is fine. It is the naive-rebuild trap (`CLAUDE.md`) in an
instrument rather than a gate: `IMPOSTOR_GIRTH_Q` is the number `impostor_of`
*solves for*, so the two agree by construction. **A hull that hits its target
tells you nothing about whether the target is right.**

## 3. The tree's real outline

Reading the tree at its EXTENT — the widest triangle centre per band, which is
what the eye sees, because a conifer's outline is the reach of its branches and
the cards out there are alpha-masked air rather than absent:

```
cargo run --release -p client --features render --example hull_profile
```

| | vs quantile | **vs extent** |
|---|---|---|
| silhouette area | 89–91 % | **65–72 %** |
| per-band width, canopy bands | 0.85–0.96 | **0.53–0.76** |

The swap drops roughly a third of the tree's visual mass and a quarter to a
half of its width. Both profiles are printed side by side now so the next
reader cannot make the first mistake.

## 4. The rationale under the knob had expired, on a date

`IMPOSTOR_GIRTH_Q`'s doc justified 0.9 with *"the 10 % outside it is branch
tips that are sub-pixel at the swap distance"*. That sentence was written when
a conifer was 7 m tall. **Forest scale v0 (2026-09-14) doubled it and nobody
re-derived the sentence.** The trimmed band at the 55 m swap
(`MEDIUM_TREE_LOD_SWAP_M`), on a 720-row frame over a 60° vertical FOV:

```
  variant 0:   1.6   7.6   6.6   5.7   5.3   4.3   4.2   5.4   (px per side)
  variant 4:   1.4   6.2   8.6  11.0  11.7   6.6   5.7   4.5
```

**4–12 px per side**, 8–24 px across the tree. Not sub-pixel — a visible ring
of missing canopy around every far tree.

The *other* half of that doc is still true and is why the fix is not `max`: a
stray ground-level card sits far outside its band's mass, so the widest vertex
builds a drum. Band 0 already reads 0.9–2.9× the tree's width at q = 0.9.

## 5. Why the two changes are one change

The sweep, target share of the tree's extent per quantile (the lathe then
loses a further ~10 % to ring smoothing and the tip closing to a point — which
is why 0.90 lands at the 65–72 % measured in §3):

| q | 0.90 | 0.93 | 0.95 | 0.97 | **0.99** | 1.00 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| share of extent | 0.73–0.81 | 0.77–0.85 | 0.80–0.87 | 0.83–0.91 | **0.90–0.98** | 1.00 |

⚠ **0.97 was picked first and the gate refused it.** `× 0.90` for the lathe's
own loss puts variant 4 at **74 %** against the 75 % floor — the projection was
right and landed on the wrong side of the line. The floor is what must not
move (one that both 0.65 and 0.74 pass is not a gate), so the quantile did.

`max` stays refused, but **the reason had to be re-checked rather than
inherited**: at band 0 the tree's extent and its quantile radius are EQUAL on
every variant (0.198/0.198, 1.288/1.288, 0.204/0.206 …), so the stray
ground-level card the old doc describes is not there to reach for and 0.99
builds no drum. That is a property of today's trees, not of the rule, which is
why the rule keeps the trim.

**Widening alone would have made it worse.** A wider OPAQUE hull is a fatter
smooth capsule — more of exactly the thing the frame objects to. The radius is
only safe to spend once the hull is porous, so the material change is the
load-bearing half and the quantile rides on it:

- the hull takes `canopy_material(variant, key)` — the same alpha-masked,
  double-sided needle or leaf card the near tree already wears — where it wore
  untextured opaque `foliage`. Closes `NOW.md` §0out item 1 and `ART.md`
  rule 6 together, and makes the swap a detail change rather than a species
  change.
- `IMPOSTOR_GIRTH_Q` 0.9 → 0.97.

Move one without the other and the number is wrong again; the doc on the
constant says so.

## 6. The gate, and the mutant

`tests/tree.rs::the_impostor_keeps_the_tree_s_outline` measures the silhouette
against the tree's own extent, floor 0.75.

`the_impostor_stands_inside_the_tree_it_replaces` stays — it is the sim's
arithmetic, `SPAWN_CLEAR_M`'s half — but it **could not have caught this**: it
compares two scalars and reads 0.49–0.59 whether the hull is a faithful
profile or a capsule. It was green over the whole defect.

⚠ **Run the mutant** (`CLAUDE.md`): the new gate must fail on the 0.9 this
shipped with. A floor both values pass is not a gate.

## 8. ⚠ The material half was a REGRESSION and is reverted

**Written after looking at the frame it produced, which is the only reason it
was caught.** The arithmetic above was right and the fix it implied was wrong.

`canopy_material` on the hull made the far forest **skeletal** — thin scraggly
wisps where there had been smooth capsules. Worse, plainly, in the same
vantage.

The mechanism should have been predicted from a number already in this tree:
`tree::needle_image` is a **sprig**, held at the fern card's own **0.192 /
0.255** coverage (`NOW.md` §0t item 7). That is correct for a canopy, where a
dozen cards overlap into a mass. Stretched once over a hull it deletes three
quarters of the hull's surface. **Coverage is scale-invariant**, so no
`uv_scale` recovers it — more tiles of a 20 %-covered card is still 20 %.

So: a sprig card is the wrong texture for an impostor. What a hull wants is a
**baked silhouette of the whole tree** — `TERRAIN.md` §4's octahedral
billboard — which has the tree's own alpha, mostly opaque in the core and
ragged only at the edge. That is a slice, not a material swap.

**What survives the revert:** the measurement (§2–§4), the gate, and
`IMPOSTOR_GIRTH_Q = 0.99`, which the gate holds and which is justified by the
tree's own extent regardless of what the hull wears. **What does not:** the
claim in §5 that widening is only safe on a porous hull. It is the right
worry and it is now untested in the direction that matters — the shipped hull
is opaque AND wide, and whether that reads as a fatter plastic capsule is a
question for the next frame, not for this note.

⚠ **The general shape, for whoever fixes this properly:** an alpha coverage
figure is a property of the CARD, and reusing a card across two geometries
reuses its coverage into a context that never agreed to it. Same family as
this repo's "a rule about feet is not a rule about rays".

## 9. What the quantile actually bought, on screen

The opaque-and-wide hull — what ships after §8's revert — captured on the same
vantage and seed, against the same crop of the hull band at 60–120 m:

| | green pixels in the hull band |
|---|---|
| `IMPOSTOR_GIRTH_Q` 0.9 | 17.5 % |
| `IMPOSTOR_GIRTH_Q` 0.99 | **18.4 %** |

**A 5 % relative change, where §5's arithmetic predicted ~20 % more
silhouette.** Both numbers are right and the gap between them is the lesson:
`∫ 2r dy` is the side-on area of one solid of revolution in ISOLATION, and a
stand is a pile of overlapping ones seen in projection. Widening objects that
already occlude each other does not buy proportional screen coverage.

So the honest verdict on the whole pass: the quantile is **correct and
marginal**. The hull is the right width now and it still reads as a smooth
faceted capsule, because width was never the dominant term — shape and surface
are, and both wait on §8's baked billboard.

⚠ **Carry this into the next measurement**: a 3D quantity about one object is
not a prediction about a frame full of them. The screen number is cheap
(`PIL`, one crop, a green-dominance mask) and should have been taken first.

## 7. What this does NOT fix

- The hull is still a lathe. `TERRAIN.md` §4's octahedral billboard is the
  real answer to a tree at distance and is still unbuilt.
- `IMPOSTOR_SIDES = 7` still facets at close range; the frames show it.
- The alpha-masked, double-sided material costs a masked pass and doubles
  fragments over `foliage` on ~1,260 outer-ring hulls. `tests/tree.rs` gates
  triangles, not draw cost, and **nothing here measures frame time on a GPU.**
