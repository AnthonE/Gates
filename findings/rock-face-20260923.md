# Rock face v0: cliffs stop being one pale sheet, 2026-09-23

The operator's words were *"our world is still weak terrain wise"*. This box's
first in-game capture showed where. At the pinned `dev_spawn = 1500,600`,
`0-design.png` is two-thirds scarp: one shelf edge of `terrain::REMAP_LUT`,
about 20 m of ground at 45–55°, rendered as a single pale value with the
texture of wet plaster. `1-west`, `3-east` and `5-sky` all have the same
smooth pale dome on the skyline. The splat is correct: the cliff band forces
rock and rock is granite at luma 147, the centre of `ART.md` §3's band. What
the scarp lacks is structure. A smooth heightfield lights as a smooth
heightfield, and the rock photograph tiles at 4 m and averages to its mean
well before 30 m.

## What landed

This is a procedural rock face in `ground_splat.wgsl`, applied wherever the
classifier's rock weight is non-zero:

- a jittered 3D block lattice in world space (7 m), read through a 1.6 m
  warp;
- a per-block facet lean (0.38) and a finer lattice inside it (2.2 m, 0.16);
- a key per *pair* of blocks, so that 30 % of boundaries are cracks;
- weathering at 14 m;
- streaks on faces steeper than about 37°.

All 16 numbers travel in the uniform (`rock_a`–`rock_d`) and are listed in
the `DECISIONS.md` row. Everything fades with the size of a block on screen,
measured from the fragment's own footprint before any branch.

## The iterations, each captured and each cut for a reason

| version | what it was | what the capture said |
|---|---|---|
| v1 | 3.2 m blocks, a crack on every boundary, and blocks deciding rock against turf | **crazy paving**, and the foot of the scarp strewn with white paving stones laid on grass |
| v2 | 7 m + 2.2 m facets, sparse cracks, weathering, streaks; no weight moved | rock at last, but the polygons read as camouflage |
| v3 | the lattice read through a warp, stronger streaks | natural; the dark patches read as stains up close and as jointed rock across a valley |
| v4 | pillowed blocks with a soft dark joint | a **dry-stone wall**. Cut |
| v5 | v3 with odd, brightness-neutral streaks | shipped |

Two of these lessons generalise:

- **An outline round every block reads as masonry, whatever its width.**
  That held for the thin cracks in v1 and for the soft pillowed joints in v4.
  What reads as rock is a *minority* of boundaries cracked and the rest
  carried by lighting alone.
- **A splat decision made per block becomes a paving stone on flat ground.**
  The block lattice is 3D, so where the terrain flattens a block's slice is a
  polygon lying on the grass. Deciding rock against turf belongs to the
  classifier. Scree at a scarp's foot is §0rock item 3's seeding, not a
  shader's call.

## Measured

The scarp region of `0-design.png` (columns 40–1150, rows 10–220),
Rec.601 luma:

| frame | mean | sd | 8 px-block sd | neighbour |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| before | 134.9 | 13.4 | 11.2 | 6.40 |
| v3 (streaks darken only) | 130.0 | 16.9 | 14.9 | 6.22 |
| v5 (shipped) | 133.7 | 16.9 | 14.9 | 6.25 |

The 8 px-block sd is the contrast at the scale a face is read at, rather
than the grain's, and it rose **+33 %**. v3 took the region's mean down
**3.6 %** because its streaks only darkened. v5's streak term is odd about 0.5
over a symmetric lattice, and the mean came back to **−0.9 %** (the cracks,
plus two live runs that do not repeat wind or clutter animation).
`tests/rock_face.rs` now holds that form.

## What it did not do

These are listed in `NOW.md` §0rf:
- the silhouette against the sky is still the heightfield's own;
- the lip and the foot of a scarp are still a contour line;
- nobody has looked at it on a GPU. Every frame here is lavapipe at 1280×720.
